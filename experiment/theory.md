Overrun in ice cream refers to the percentage increase in volume due to the incorporation of air during the freezing process. It is an important quality parameter that influences the texture, body, appearance, and palatability of ice cream. The principle of this experiment is based on measuring the difference between the volume of frozen ice cream and the volume of melted ice cream after removing entrapped air. When ice cream is melted, the incorporated air escapes, resulting in a reduced volume. This difference is used to calculate the percentage overrun. Proper control of overrun ensures desirable quality, whereas too much or too little air adversely affects the product characteristics.
